A Glimpse Into Gospel Hill

By Knox Aluise


Rocks Remember

When I walk through the woods, I am reminded that death is all around us. I see it as I walk past a spider web with a fly caught in it. I hear it in the crunch of dead leaves I walk on.

Death is something that I have always struggled to contend with. The idea of being gone from this world, and then eventually forgotten, is especially unsettling to me.

One day, I was hiking on Penn State Behrend's campus, which has stretches and streches of forested area on its property. I've always loved hiking, so exploring woods on campus was something I'd do from time to time. In one of these instances, I found a trail I'd never seen before, and just like many other times, I decided to check it out. I stopped when I stood before a strange rock structure. It had a small fence around it made of cord and wood. Staked next to it was a US flag, with an emblem attatched to it that said "US Vertern". It looked like the same kind of flags they put next to grave stones that belong to veterns, though usually the emblem specifies which war they fought in. I didn't know what to make of it, I guessed it was perhaps a student-created vetern memorial. I thought it was weird to be in the middle of the woods like this, though.



I continued on the trail, until I stumbled upon a sign that said "Gospel Hill Burial Ground" and my interest was piqued.

Of The Hill

There is not a lot of information online about this burial cite. Most of the information I was able to find was from a plastic sign that sits right outside of the cemetary.

Walking into the graveyard, it at first looks unassuming. There is an array of flora and fauna that makes it difficult to see some of the graves from a distance.

But as you continue to walk into the site, you start to see more and more.


Shockingly, there are some graves that are not only still standing, but they are legible as well.


But most of the graves are broken.


Although the forest around Gospel Hill buzzes with life, the site itself has an odd sense of peace to it. The people who were buried There do not have duties to fulfill, people to take care of. They did all of those things many years ago, and now they lay and rest. I can feel that sentiment as I walk around the graves, the wind sloftly blowing through the leaves.


Gone But Not Forgotten

The stone pile memorial that I mentioned at the beginning of this ancidote has a plaque that sits next to it. There, it explains what it is memorializing.

I am always astounded by how things never truly go away. Most people would expect a cemetary that was abandoned for 60 years would become part of the dirt, but it stood and presisted until it was remembered again. And now over a hundred years later it is still watched over and preserved.
